## 10.2 Nonconformity and Corrective Action

For each significant nonconformity, Plus Sarl:

1. Analyzes the cause of the problem through exchanges with the customer and the industrial partner.
2. Identifies whether the cause is:
   - production defect
   - human error
   - labeling problem
   - logistics problem
   - lack of initial clarification
3. Defines an appropriate corrective action.

Corrective actions may include:

- strengthening quality control
- reminder of technical requirements
- implementation of a specific attention note at the supplier
- improvement of specification clarification
- modification of an internal process

In the case of repetitive nonconformity, an in-depth analysis is carried out to prevent any recurrence.

The effectiveness of corrective actions is verified during subsequent productions.

A corrective action register will be implemented in FileMaker or in a document at the supplier, distinguishing:

- supplier nonconformities
- carrier nonconformities
- nonconformities related to misuse or client clarification

## 10.3 Continual Improvement

Plus Sarl continually improves its quality management system in order to ensure:

- lasting customer satisfaction
- compliance with deadlines
- reduction of nonconformities
- management of industrial partners
- stability and reliability of operations

The effectiveness of improvements is evaluated based on:

- the absence of significant complaints
- compliance with announced deadlines
- the stability of customer orders
- the maintenance of lasting relationships with partners

### Improvement Triggers

Improvements may be initiated following:

- a nonconformity
- a logistics delay
- customer feedback
- the analysis of performance indicators
- observations made during on-site visits at industrial partners in China

Regular trips to China constitute a major lever for continual improvement, enabling:

- strengthening communication
- optimizing production processes
- improving the understanding of quality requirements
- adjusting control and monitoring procedures

### Verification of Effectiveness

An improvement is considered effective when:

- delays decrease
- nonconformities do not recur
- exchanges are smoother
- customers maintain their confidence and their orders

Improvement decisions are consolidated during the annual management review.

## 9.3 Management Review (Enhanced Version)

### 9.3 Management Review

Management carries out at least one annual review of the quality management system to ensure:

- its relevance;
- its adequacy;
- its effectiveness;
- its alignment with the company's strategy.

### 1. Review Input Elements

The management review takes into account:

**a) Indicator Results (9.1)**

- Compliance with deadlines;
- Number and nature of nonconformities;
- Supplier performance;
- Customer satisfaction level.

**b) Status of Corrective Actions (10.2)**

- Analysis of significant nonconformities;
- Actions implemented;
- Verification of their effectiveness.

**c) Status of Risks and Opportunities (6.1)**

- Risks related to industrial partners;
- Logistics risks;
- Risks related to deadlines;
- Identified improvement opportunities.

**d) Performance of Industrial Partners (8.4)**

- Reliability;
- Responsiveness;
- Stability of the relationship.

**e) Resources (Chapter 7)**

- Adequacy of digital tools;
- Possible need for process reinforcement;
- Internal organization.

**f) Improvement Opportunities (10.3)**

- Improvements implemented;
- Improvements planned;
- Results of visits to China.

### 2. Decisions and Actions from the Review

The management review allows:

- defining new quality objectives if necessary;
- deciding on corrective or preventive actions;
- strengthening supplier selection or monitoring;
- adjusting operational processes;
- planning priority improvements.

Decisions are formalized in a management review report.

### 3. Frequency

The management review is carried out at least once a year.
Intermediate reviews may be conducted in the event of a major occurrence.
